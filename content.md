## Slide 2 — What is Prompt Engineering?

**What to say:**

Before we get into the demo, let us establish what a prompt actually is.

A **prompt** is the instruction or question you type into an AI system like ChatGPT. **Prompt engineering** is the skill of crafting that instruction precisely so that the AI gives you the most useful, accurate, and relevant response possible.

Here is a simple way to think about it: the AI model is a powerful engine. Your prompt is the steering wheel. Without good steering, even the most powerful engine goes nowhere useful.

The definition on screen says it well — *"Prompt engineering is the discipline of crafting, refining, and optimising inputs given to AI language models to produce more accurate, relevant, and useful outputs."*

Every prompt has two parts:
- **The Input** — your instruction, which combines the task, context, audience, and format.
- **The Output** — the AI response, which is entirely shaped by how well you wrote the input.

Look at the example on the right: *"Explain how electricity is generated in a thermal power plant in simple terms for a high school student."*

Notice it has an action (Explain), a context (thermal power plant), a tone (simple terms), and an audience (high school student). That structure is what makes a prompt powerful.

---

## Slide 3 — Why Prompts Matter

**What to say:**

So why does this matter so much? Because the same AI model can give you a completely useless answer or a completely expert-level answer — depending purely on how you phrase your question.

Look at the six reasons on screen:

1. **Precision** — Specific prompts give targeted answers. Vague ones force the AI to guess, and it returns shallow content.
2. **Efficiency** — A well-written prompt gets you the answer in one shot instead of five back-and-forth follow-ups.
3. **Context Control** — Adding who the answer is for, and what the purpose is, turns a generic response into a personalised one.
4. **Output Quality** — The model is constant. Your prompt is the variable. Better prompt, better result.
5. **Structured Control** — You can dictate tone, format, depth, and length — making AI a fully customisable tool.
6. **AI Collaboration** — Prompt engineering is the new literacy. It is the primary skill separating effective AI users from those who get mediocre results.

The statistics on screen back this up:
- **76%** better outcomes with specific prompts
- **3x** faster task completion
- **#1** most in-demand AI skill in 2025
- **40%** productivity gain in knowledge work

These are not small differences. This is why learning to write good prompts is one of the most valuable skills you can develop right now. I will now hand over to Mithun.

## Slide 8 — Why the Specific Prompt Succeeded

**What to say:**

So what exactly made the specific prompt work so well? There were four deliberate decisions inside that one sentence.

**1. Context and Subject Clarity**
We told the AI exactly which aspect of electricity to cover — thermal power plant generation and grid distribution. This eliminated any guesswork about which corner of a huge topic to address.

**2. Defined Audience**
The phrase *"high school student"* calibrated everything — vocabulary, analogies, and complexity level. The AI chose relatable comparisons, like comparing the turbine to a jet engine, and avoided technical jargon that would confuse a student.

**3. Scope Boundaries**
Asking for the complete journey from fuel burning to home delivery set a clear start and end point. No wasted detours into solar power or nuclear energy — just the defined process, fully covered.

**4. Implicit Format Signal**
The phrase *"explain in simple terms"* implicitly asked for a structured, step-by-step breakdown. The AI delivered exactly that instead of a dense unreadable paragraph.

The result — the specific prompt outperformed the vague one by **3.4 times** across all quality dimensions.

## Slide 9 — Key Elements of a Good Prompt

**What to say:**

From our experiment, we identified a simple four-part framework. We call it the **CATO formula**.

**C — Context**
Provide the situation or background. The AI cannot read your mind. Give it enough information to anchor its reasoning in the right domain. Example: *"...in a thermal power plant..."*

**A — Action**
Start with a precise action verb. Explain, Compare, Summarise, List, Write, Analyse. Vague verbs produce vague results. Specific verbs unlock structured outputs.

**T — Target Audience**
Who is the response for? Define the reader's expertise, age, or role. This single variable controls vocabulary, tone, analogies, and depth. Example: *"...for a high school student."*

**O — Output Format**
Tell the AI how the response should look. Bullet points, numbered steps, a table, under 200 words. Structure guides both readability and completeness.

Put it together:
> **Context + Action + Target Audience + Output Format = Professional-grade AI response every time.**

Most people skip three of these four elements — and that is why they get mediocre results. I will now hand over to Naren for the final section.

## Slide 10 — Prompt Writing Tips and Best Practices

**What to say:**

Thank you Harsha. I am Naren, and I will cover the practical tips you can start using immediately.

These are eight best practices for writing better prompts:

1. **Be Specific, Not Vague.** Replace "tell me about X" with "explain Y aspect of X for Z audience in N format." Every added detail narrows the AI's search space and improves the output.

2. **Assign a Role.** Start your prompt with "Act as a [role]" — for example, "Act as a senior physics professor." This frames the AI's expertise and tone and dramatically improves depth.

3. **Specify Output Format.** Ask for bullet points, numbered steps, a table, or even JSON. Structured requests produce structured, readable results.

4. **Use Few-Shot Examples.** Show the AI exactly what you want. "Write 3 taglines. Like this: *Power your world with clean energy.*" Showing beats telling.

5. **Iterate and Refine.** Treat it as a conversation. After the first response, say "Make step 3 more detailed" or "Simplify the vocabulary." You do not have to get it right in one go.

6. **Set Explicit Constraints.** Define what NOT to include. "No jargon, no equations, no more than 150 words." Negative constraints prevent the AI from going off-topic or over-complicating.

7. **Use Chain-of-Thought.** For complex problems, add *"Think step by step before answering."* This forces the model to reason systematically and reduces errors.

8. **Test and Benchmark.** Run the same question with three different phrasings and compare results. Prompt engineering is a scientific process — measure, compare, improve.
